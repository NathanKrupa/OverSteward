## 1. Market Intelligence

### 1.1. The Underserved Middle

The Almoner's total addressable market: approximately **100,000–120,000 U.S. nonprofits** in the $500K–$5M revenue band. This segment is the most strategically underserved in the fundraising consulting industry — too large to operate on instinct alone, too small to afford CCS or Bridgespan.

- 97% of all nonprofits have budgets under $5M
- 76% have revenue under $100K (too small for consulting)
- ~8% fall in the $500K–$5M sweet spot
- Only 3% exceed $5M (served by major firms)

### 1.2. The Customer Profile

**The Organization:**
- 501(c)(3) with annual revenue of $500K–$5M
- Mission: human services (food banks, shelters, community services), education, or faith-based
- Located in the South, Southeast, or rural Midwest (highest density of ideal customers)
- Community-based, locally governed, 5–50 employees plus significant volunteer labor
- Revenue mix: individual donations, some government funding, limited foundation grants
- Likely a member of a network (Feeding America, Catholic Charities, local United Way)

**The Decision-Maker:**
- Executive Director/CEO, often founder or long-tenured leader, median age 54
- Wearing multiple hats — programs, fundraising, community relations, board management
- May have no development director, or one who is new (within 16 months), part-time, or dual-hatted
- Motivated by mission, not career advancement
- Searching for help at 11 PM, not during business hours
- Values practical guidance over theory; wary of consultants who don't understand their world
- Budget-conscious — can invest $500–$5,000 in consulting, not $50,000

**What They Search For:**
- "How to write a foundation grant"
- "Grant writing for food banks"
- "Fundraising plan for small nonprofit"
- "How to find foundation grants"
- "Nonprofit fundraising consultant affordable"

### 1.3. The Development Director Crisis

This is the Herald's most powerful marketing angle:

- Average development director tenure: **16 months**
- Half anticipate leaving within two years
- 40% unsure they'll stay in fundraising at all
- Replacement cost: **90%–200% of salary**
- Mean time to fill a vacancy at a small nonprofit: **21 months**
- 25% of development roles go unfilled each year
- Larger organizations systematically poach the best talent with higher salaries

**What this means for the Herald:** At any given moment, a large percentage of organizations in the target band either have no development director, have one about to leave, or have one too new to be effective. The Almoner is the experienced development professional these organizations can access without the revolving door.

### 1.4. The Culture of Tired

Small and midsize nonprofits run on tired. Nearly 75% report persistent job vacancies. Workloads shift to remaining staff, causing burnout and further departures. One-third of nonprofits in Florida have no reserve funds at all.

The Herald speaks to this exhaustion with empathy, not additional demands. Marketing that says "do these 12 things" to an ED who is already doing 47 things will be ignored. Marketing that says "here is one thing that will help, and it takes ten minutes" earns attention.

### 1.5. Why Now: Five Converging Forces

1. **Federal funding cuts** — SNAP, LFPA ($420M cancelled), and other cuts push fundraising burden onto local organizations that never had to fundraise aggressively
2. **AI disruption in grant writing** — Organizations are searching for tools that demystify the grant process; the Grant Helper is positioned at this intersection
3. **Development director scarcity** — 21-month vacancies and 25% unfilled roles create sustained demand for external strategic guidance
4. **Donor base erosion** — 7% drop in donors in H1 2022; declining small-dollar donors force diversification into foundation fundraising
5. **Post-pandemic fatigue** — After years of crisis response, leaders want sustainable systems, not heroic one-time efforts

### 1.6. Geographic Focus

The highest density of ideal customers is in the **South, Southeast, and lower Midwest** — particularly Georgia, South Carolina, Tennessee, Alabama, North Carolina, and Florida. This is home territory for Nathan. These regions have:

- Higher concentrations of religious and human services organizations
- Community engagement that runs through faith and relational networks
- Cultural invisibility to major metro-based consulting firms

The Herald prioritizes speaking engagements, network relationships, and content that resonates with this geography first.

## 3. The Five KPIs: The Herald's Dashboard

Ordered as a funnel. The Herald's diagnostic discipline: identify the weakest link and focus effort there.

### 3.1. Visibility: Website Visitors per Month
- **Sources**: Organic search, social referrals, direct traffic, email click-throughs, speaking engagement follow-up
- **Target**: Baseline in Month 1, then 10% month-over-month growth to 1,000 monthly visitors
- **Measurement**: Google Analytics (Property 258447004). Organic baseline: February 22, 2026.

### 3.2. Capture: Email List Size and Growth Rate
- **Key metric**: Visitor-to-subscriber conversion rate
- **Target**: 3-5% conversion rate
- **Diagnostics**: Is the lead magnet compelling? Is the opt-in placement effective? Is the value proposition clear in under 10 seconds?

### 3.3. Nurture: Email Open Rate and Click Rate
- **Targets**: Open rate 35%+, click rate 3%+
- **Diagnostics**: Are subject lines earning attention? Is content delivering genuine value? Are subscribers dropping at specific points?

### 3.4. Conversion: Discovery Call Bookings per Month
- **Target**: 2-4 discovery calls per month within the first year
- **Diagnostics**: Is there a clear path from email to booking? Do subscribers know what a discovery call *is*?

### 3.5. Revenue: Consulting Engagement Income
- **Target**: Establish baseline and trajectory (revenue targets are Chestertron's domain)
- **Diagnostics**: Are calls converting? Is pricing value-based (per Enns)? Are we attracting the right clients?
- **Pricing range**: $500–$5,000 per engagement. Potential $49–$99/month subscription for ongoing guidance. Grant Helper remains free (top-of-funnel).

### 3.6. The Failure Protocol
If any KPI shows no improvement for 90 consecutive days despite consistent effort, the Herald escalates to Chestertron with a written diagnostic: what was tried, what the data shows, and three alternative hypotheses. A show that isn't selling tickets needs a new poster, a new venue, or a new act — not louder shouting at the same empty street.

## 4. The Marketing Rhythm: Designed for Low Discipline

Nathan's Clifton Strengths: high Learner/Ideation/Intellection, low Discipline. The rhythm is designed accordingly.

**Three principles:**
1. **Batch, don't drip**: Quarterly content sprints, not daily habits.
2. **Automate the repeatable**: Kit handles delivery. The Grant Helper works 24/7. Schedule in batches.
3. **Ritualize the review**: Monday, 15 minutes, one action.

### 4.1. The Weekly Review (Monday, 15 Minutes)
1. Open KPI dashboard (2 min)
2. Note significant movement up or down (2 min)
3. Identify weakest funnel stage (3 min)
4. Define ONE action to address it (3 min)
5. Record in Todoist with deadline (2 min)
6. Close dashboard, proceed with day (3 min)

### 4.2. The Quarterly Content Sprint (One Focused Day)
1. Review StoryBrand framework for needed updates
2. Brainstorm 8-12 content topics mapped to content taxonomy (see Section 7)
3. Write 3-4 blog posts or articles in draft
4. Schedule for publication over the coming quarter
5. Review and refresh evergreen email sequence if needed

## 5. The Guerrilla Doctrine: Zero-Budget Marketing

### 5.1. The Budget Constraint as Design Principle

**The House's marketing budget is effectively zero.** This is not a limitation — it is the primary design constraint. Any recommendation requiring significant cash outlay must be justified against zero-cost alternatives. Paid advertising is not proposed until organic channels are fully exploited and funnel conversion rates justify the investment.

The funnel runs on:
- Free tools (Grant Helper)
- Writing Nathan already does
- Speaking engagements that pay in leads, not fees
- A $39/month Kit subscription
- SEO that compounds at zero marginal cost
- Peer referrals earned through demonstrated expertise
- Network leverage (Feeding America's 200 food banks + 60,000 partner agencies)

### 5.2. Channel Priority (Ordered by ROI)

**Tier 1 — The Stage (Nathan's highest leverage)**
1. **Conference speaking**: Feeding America events, state food bank associations, Catholic nonprofit gatherings, diocesan development offices. Prioritize Southeast: GA, SC, TN, AL, NC, FL. Zero cost. Maximum authority. Nathan's strongest suit.
2. **Podcast guesting**: The nonprofit podcast ecosystem is hungry for practitioners who have actually raised money. Pre-qualified audience at zero cost.
3. **Webinars**: Kit supports natively. Feed directly into email funnel.
4. **Parish and diocesan talks**: Catholic nonprofit network runs on personal relationships. One talk > a hundred blog posts.
5. **Network hub presentations**: A single endorsement or referral from a Feeding America hub, state food bank association, or United Way reaches hundreds of organizations simultaneously.

**Tier 2 — The Written Word**
6. **Blog posts** (TheAlmoner.com): SEO-driven, targeting the search terms the customer actually uses (see Section 1.2). Compound over time.
7. **External publications**: Catholic Stand, The Southern Cross, NCR. Already marketing — ensure they link to funnel.
8. **The email sequence**: 30-week evergreen nurture. Runs automatically.

**Tier 3 — The Systems (Built once, run forever)**
9. **The Grant Helper**: Lead magnet running 24/7.
10. **SEO optimization**: Rank Math, keyword targeting, internal linking.
11. **The landing page**: Conversion-optimized entry point.

**Tier 4 — Under Evaluation**
12. **Social media**: See Section 5.4.

### 5.3. Cost Per Acquisition

In a zero-budget model, "cost" is Nathan's time:
- **Speaking engagement**: ~4 hours. Target: 2-5 signups or 1 discovery call.
- **Blog post**: ~3 hours. Target: compound organic traffic over 6-12 months.
- **Email sequence**: ~40 hours to build (one-time). Target: automated nurture indefinitely.
- **Grant Helper**: Built. Continuous low-friction conversion.
- **Network referral**: Relationship maintenance. Highest-converting, lowest-cost channel when it works.

If a channel's time-cost-per-lead exceeds speaking or writing, it is deprioritized.

### 5.4. Social Media Doctrine

Tier 4 — Under Evaluation. The Enns discipline: don't scatter forces.

**Testing framework (when ready):**
1. Pick ONE platform (LinkedIn is the likely candidate for nonprofit professionals)
2. 90-day experiment with measurable KPI (click-throughs, subscriber conversions)
3. Batch content creation (repurpose blog posts and speaking clips)
4. At 90 days: producing results? Systematize. Not? Stop without guilt.

No social presence maintained for vanity metrics without funnel contribution.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

### 6.1. The Big Firms (Not Competition)
CCS Fundraising, Bridgespan, Campbell & Company, BWF, Marts & Lundy, Schultz & Williams. These serve universities, hospitals, major cultural organizations. Fees start in tens of thousands, scale to six figures. The $500K–$5M segment is invisible to them.

### 6.2. The Mid-Tier (Adjacent)
Donorly (offers "Three-Month Jumpstart"), Aly Sterling Philanthropy, Sprout Fundraising (Indianapolis — affordable tools for small nonprofits, emphasis on reducing dev director turnover). These are the closest analogs. The Herald monitors them quarterly.

### 6.3. The Four Gaps the Almoner Fills
1. **Faith-based specialization with intellectual depth.** The Covenant Group and AmPhil serve faith-based orgs, but the Almoner's CST grounding and ethical AI framework offer a different philosophical foundation — one that resonates with organizations motivated by vocation.
2. **Food banking and hunger-specific expertise.** Very few consultants combine deep food banking operational knowledge with fundraising strategy. Nathan's 14+ years and $15M+ at Golden Harvest is a rare credential.
3. **Affordable, scalable digital delivery.** The Grant Helper, the 30-week email sequence, and the content strategy allow the Almoner to serve organizations that cannot afford a $50,000 engagement but desperately need guidance.
4. **Rural and Southern cultural fluency.** Most major firms are headquartered in major metros. The $500K–$5M organizations in rural Georgia, the Carolinas, Alabama, Mississippi, and Tennessee are culturally invisible to them.

### 6.4. Monitoring Protocol (Quarterly)
Scan for: new AI grant tools for small nonprofits, Catholic fundraising consultancies launching digital, Feeding America network tools overlapping Grant Helper.

Framework:
- **Ignore**: Different audience or different problem.
- **Differentiate**: Partial overlap. Sharpen messaging around distinctions.
- **Respond**: Direct competitor. Escalate to Chestertron.

### 6.5. The Anti-Fragility Principle
Long-term defense is not features — it is Nathan's reputation and relationships. A 14-year track record, a published body of work, a network of food bank directors, and genuine CST conviction cannot be replicated by a startup with better software.

### 9.1. Testimonial Collection
After every engagement, speaking event, or substantive Grant Helper interaction, request a brief testimonial. The ask is specific: "Would you share one sentence about what this did for your organization?" Specific outcomes, not generic endorsements.

### 9.2. Case Study Development
Document consulting results as case studies following StoryBrand structure: character's problem, guide's plan, action taken, failure avoided, success achieved. Names used only with explicit permission. Prioritize stories that demonstrate the development director gap being bridged — this is the most resonant narrative.

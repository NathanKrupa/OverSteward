# Fundraising.AI Framework for Responsible and Beneficial AI

The Almoner, LLC / Golden Harvest Food Bank are signatories of this framework.
Source: https://fundraising.ai/framework/
Public commitment: https://thealmoner.com/responsible-ai/
Signed: February 2026 | Review cadence: biannual | Next review: August 2026

The framework addresses ethical AI use in the nonprofit fundraising sector, which represents
nearly $500 billion annually in the United States. It is reviewed biannually to address
technological advances. Signatories commit to supporting the framework "to the best of our
ability and knowledge" across their respective roles within the fundraising profession.

## Foundation Requirement

Organizations must establish comprehensive data-security and governance policies before any AI
deployment, ensuring compliance, consent management, and risk mitigation.

## The 10 Tenets

### 1. Privacy and Security

Safeguard personal and sensitive data through robust security standards, maintaining compliance
with data protection regulations. Requirements include:
- Consent management
- Data use control
- Processing restrictions
- Right to correction
- Right to deletion
- Data accessibility
- Objection rights
- Privacy law adherence

### 2. Data Ethics

Ensure compliance with local, regional, and international data protection standards aligned
with human rights and ethical AI principles, using accurate, relevant data collected with
proper consent.

### 3. Inclusiveness

Address biases and disparities throughout the AI lifecycle by monitoring and evaluating
systems for:
- Non-discrimination and bias prevention
- Representative, high-quality data
- Fairness and equality
- Inclusive impact and design

### 4. Accountability

Share responsibility for AI applications to ensure alignment with organizational missions and
values. Systems must be:
- Verifiable and replicable
- Auditable
- Appealable
- Remediable
- Liable

**Critical requirement:** Any AI system that makes critical decisions without human involvement
must be subject to ongoing human review and authorization.

### 5. Transparency and Explainability

Within reasonable IP protections, be transparent about AI development, deployment, and use.
Provide clear explanations of methodologies, results, and potential impacts. When agentic AI
affects donors or beneficiaries, consider disclosing AI involvement and explaining rationale
in accessible language.

### 6. Continuous Learning

Stay informed about Responsible and Beneficial AI developments. Incorporate best practices
into fundraising work. Educate the broader community.

### 7. Collaboration

Actively engage with peers, sharing experiences, challenges, and successes in responsible AI
for fundraising.

### 8. Legal Compliance

Remain aware of and abide by applicable laws, regulations, and best practices concerning AI
development, data protection, and fundraising operations.

### 9. Social Impact

Maximize positive social impact while minimizing harm by focusing on beneficiary needs.
Training data, objectives, and fine-tuning must align with nonprofit ethical standards,
including beneficiary dignity and consent.

**Prohibition:** AI agents must not exploit donor vulnerabilities or use undue influence,
manipulation, deception, or coercion.

### 10. Sustainability

Consider long-term sustainability and environmental impact of AI technologies. Advocate for
sustainable practices.

## Scope

This framework applies to contexts involved in fundraising and fundraising consulting —
not every project in the House of Krupa. Deploy to contexts tagged with fundraising,
grants, or donor data work. Non-fundraising contexts (game development, design, etc.)
do not need this reference.

## Practical Application

These mandates apply wherever AI touches fundraising, donor data, or beneficiary information:

1. **Minimize data exposure** — Query only the data you need. Avoid pulling entire records
   when a subset of fields suffices.
2. **No automated donor outreach** — AI must not send communications to donors without
   explicit human review and approval.
3. **Human review for decisions** — Any AI-generated analysis that could influence fundraising
   strategy, donor segmentation, or resource allocation must be reviewed by Nathan before action.
4. **No exploitation** — Analysis and content must serve the mission (feeding people, serving
   those in need), not optimize for manipulative fundraising tactics. Flag: false urgency,
   guilt amplification, beneficiary exploitation, deceptive framing.
5. **Transparency** — When AI-generated analysis or content is shared externally or used in
   donor-facing contexts, disclose AI involvement.
6. **Data stays local** — Donor and beneficiary data stays in local output files. It is not
   sent to external services, logged to cloud APIs, or included in training data.
7. **Bias awareness** — When segmenting or analyzing donors, actively consider whether the
   analysis could introduce or reinforce biases against underserved communities.

## Alignment with Catholic Social Teaching

The framework's prohibition on exploitation, manipulation, deception, and coercion (Tenet 9)
aligns directly with Catholic Social Teaching on human dignity. For the House of Krupa, this
is not merely compliance — it is mission. The partner agencies we serve (300+ churches and
faith-based nonprofits, typically volunteer-run, under $12K budgets, low tech literacy) sit
squarely in the population this framework was designed to protect.
